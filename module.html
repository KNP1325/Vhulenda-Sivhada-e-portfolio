MODULE INFORMATION

Strength of Materials 3

A structured overview of the key concepts and topics covered in Strength of Materials 3.

ABOUT THE MODULE

Building Strong Engineering Foundations

Strength of Materials 3 focuses on understanding how engineering materials behave when subjected to different types of loading and forces.

The module combines theoretical concepts, mathematical calculations, diagrams and engineering problem-solving techniques.

01

Stress and Strain

Understanding the relationship between applied forces, stress, strain and the deformation of engineering materials.

02

Axial Loading

Studying members subjected to axial tensile and compressive forces and determining their resulting stresses and deformations.

03

Torsion

Understanding the behaviour of shafts and other components subjected to twisting moments and torsional loading.

04

Bending Stress

Learning how bending moments produce stresses within structural members and how to calculate bending stresses.

05

Shear Force and Bending Moment

Developing shear force and bending moment diagrams to analyse the internal forces acting on structural members.

06

Temperature Stresses

Understanding stresses that develop when materials expand or contract because of changes in temperature.

07

Shear Stress in Beams

Understanding the distribution of shear stress within beam sections and how shear forces affect beams under different loading conditions.

08

Strain Energies

Studying the energy stored within a material as a result of deformation caused by an applied load.

09

Catenaries

Understanding the shape and behaviour of flexible cables or chains hanging under their own weight or other loads.

10

Second Moment of Area

Understanding how the distribution of an area about an axis affects a section's resistance to bending.

MY APPROACH

Turning Theory Into Problem-Solving Skills

Strength of Materials 3 requires students to understand both the theory and the calculations behind engineering problems.

During tutorials, I encourage students to understand the question first before selecting formulas and performing calculations.

“

Understand → Identify → Select → Solve → Interpret → Practise

This approach helps students develop a structured way of solving Strength of Materials problems rather than simply memorising formulas.

LEARNING OUTCOMES

What Students Should Be Able To Do

  1. Understand

    Explain important Strength of Materials concepts and principles.

  2. Analyse

    Identify the forces, stresses and loading conditions in an engineering problem.

  3. Calculate

    Apply the correct formulas and mathematical procedures to solve problems.

  4. Interpret

    Explain the meaning of calculated results within an engineering context.
